# 090 — An existing Hub moves to Syncthing 2.x only on its owner's yes, after a copy of its Syncthing

**Context.** Decision 068 keeps every Hub set up before the setup link moved new Hubs to Syncthing 2.x on the 1.x line, whose last release is 1.30.0 of July 2025. Moving such a Hub converts its database for good. Measured on real Docker on 2026-11-27 with Syncthing 2.1.7 (`syncthing/syncthing:2`): 1.x Hubs made by the setup link as it stood before #361 and then updated by today's, a stock Syncthing 2.1.7 device paired with `vaultsync-hub pair`, vaults of 309 and 50,204 files with versions, a conflict copy and ignore patterns.

- **The conversion** runs on 2.x's first start, folder by folder: 309 files in under 0.1 s, 50,204 files in 9 to 14 s on a 16-core server, and the same 50,204 in 1 min 49 s and 11 min 51 s with Syncthing's container held to 0.1 and 0.02 CPU. Syncthing logs "Still migrating folder … files=N" every 10 s and "Migration complete" at the end. The 2.x database is about 2.5 times the 1.x one (50 MB against 20 MB).
- **During the conversion** Syncthing answers every request on its GUI port with HTTP 200 and `*** Database migration in progress ***`. The container's healthcheck therefore passes, and `docker compose up -d` starts the Hub and the helper: the healthcheck never failed. What fails is whatever waits for Syncthing's API by a fixed bound. The Hub's service waits 120 s, exits and is restarted by Docker, and it was ready seconds after the conversion (five restarts in 11 min 51 s). `vaultsync-hub status` fails throughout. The setup link's readiness wait gives up after about nine minutes, and over a Hub whose `.env` already names 2.x it then prints the hint to set that line.
- **Kept:** the device ID, every folder and device with its settings, the API key, the ignore patterns, the pairing state, `.stversions` and the conflict copies. The paired device reconnected without any act on its side and was in sync again about 20 s after the conversion ended (50,204 files). A new code paired a second device afterwards.
- **What 2.x leaves of 1.x:** `index-v0.14.0.db-migrated`, the 1.x database, opened read-only and then renamed, and `config.xml.v37`. At a start, Syncthing deletes the first once it is more than 14 days old (counted from when 1.x last wrote to it) and the second after 30 days. `config.xml` is now a version 1.30.0 refuses.
- **The way back.** The copy of `syncthing/` made before the move, put back with the `.env` line removed, brought the 50,204-file Hub back on 1.30.0, in sync with its device again within 20 s. Notes created, edited and deleted under 2.x on either side stayed as they were, and no conflict copy appeared. Without a copy, putting the two leftovers back and moving `index-v2` aside did the same on both sizes. With `index-v2` left in place, a later move to 2.x logs "Old-style database present but already migrated" and runs on the 2.x database of the first move.
- **Stopped, killed or out of disk space mid-way:** the 1.x database stays as it was, `config.xml` is already 2.x's (so 1.x alone no longer starts; the copy does), a full disk makes Syncthing exit and Docker restart it while its health still reads healthy, and the next start converts from the beginning to the same files.
- **A compose file written before #361** names `syncthing/syncthing:1` itself, and the `.env` line alone does nothing.

**Decision.**

1. Nothing moves a Hub to 2.x without its owner's yes: no run of the setup link, no image update. Decision 068 stands for everything else.
2. The move is one step. `docs/hub.md` → Move your Hub to Syncthing 2.x describes it for a person. The setup link is to ask it as a question beside the newer-image offer and carry it out, under points 4 to 8, in a change of its own.
3. The step, in order:
   1. check that Syncthing runs 1.x, that `.env` has no `VAULTSYNC_HUB_SYNCTHING_IMAGE` line, and that `docker-compose.yml` reads that line (if not, the setup link, run once, writes the current file and keeps 1.x);
   2. check that the disk has room for a copy of `syncthing/` and for a new database up to three times the old one;
   3. download `syncthing/syncthing:2` first, so a failure changes nothing;
   4. stop the stack;
   5. copy `syncthing/` beside it in the Hub's directory, with the stack stopped;
   6. add `VAULTSYNC_HUB_SYNCTHING_IMAGE=syncthing/syncthing:2` to `.env`;
   7. start the stack, and wait for the conversion.

   On the page, the commands of step 3.4 to 3.6 and of each way back are joined so that each runs only when the one before it worked: a copy that fails never leads to the move, an existing copy is never copied into or over, and a second run by mistake does nothing. The `.env` line is appended after a newline, because an `.env` edited by hand may not end in one.
4. **Consent, in setup:** asked only for an existing Hub, on a terminal, with an `.env` in the plain shape setup writes (#216) and without the line, with compose files setup can name (decision 076), with a running Syncthing that reports 1.x, and with the room of step 3.2. Otherwise setup says why it does not ask and names the page. The words: "Your Hub's Syncthing runs 1.30.0, the last 1.x release, which gets no more fixes. Move it to Syncthing 2.x now? Syncthing converts its database on the first start, and 1.x cannot open it again. Setup first stops the Hub and copies `<dir>/syncthing` to `<dir>/syncthing-1.x-backup-<date>`: that copy is the way back. Vaults, devices, ports and pairing carry over; devices reconnect by themselves. Setup waits until the conversion is done and shows its progress. Type yes to move it [yes/N]". Only `yes`, typed out, moves the Hub; a `y` given to the image offer one line above never carries over.
5. **Order, under decision 076:** the line goes into the staged `.env` and the pull reads it; only after a pull that worked does setup stop the stack and make the copy, and only then do the staged files take their places. A failed pull leaves everything as it was; a failed stop or copy starts the stack again on the files in place and says so.
6. **Finished** means Syncthing's health endpoint answers with its own JSON instead of the migration text, and `syncthing --version` in the container reports 2.x. Until then setup waits, with no time bound, and prints Syncthing's latest "Still migrating folder" line now and then; its readiness wait follows as before. No wait in setup reads the migration text as a failure, and none prints the 1.x-on-2.x hint while Syncthing converts (the readiness wait learns this in #364). In the documented step the owner waits for "Migration complete" in `docker compose logs syncthing`, then for `vaultsync-hub status`.
7. **Not finished:** when the Syncthing container stops or restarts during the wait, or its log says "Failed to migrate", setup stops waiting, shows Syncthing's log, and says that the 1.x database is untouched and the copy holds the Hub as it was, that running the setup again once the cause is fixed (a full disk reads "database or disk is full") converts from the start, and where the way back is described. Interrupted while it waits, setup says that the conversion goes on in the container.
8. **Never:** move a Hub by itself or without a terminal; change `.env` before the images are pulled; edit an `.env` it does not edit today; move a Hub it cannot tell to be on 1.x; touch the vaults; restore the copy by itself, delete it, or delete what 2.x leaves. The copy stays until its owner removes it; the page says it may go once every device syncs on 2.x.
9. **The way back,** on the page: stop the stack, put the copy back in place of `syncthing/`, remove the line from `.env`, start. Without a copy, while both leftovers are still there: put `index-v0.14.0.db-migrated` back as `index-v0.14.0.db` and `config.xml.v37` back as `config.xml`, and move `index-v2` aside — the third move, which the page marks as the one not to forget.

**Why.** The 1.x database cannot be rebuilt from 2.x, and Syncthing deletes its own leftover after two weeks or sooner. A copy taken before the move, with nothing writing to it, is the only way back that holds for good, and the one tested with changes on both sides. Stopping the stack first is what makes the copy whole. Pulling first keeps a network failure from leaving a stopped Hub. Waiting on the migration text rather than on time follows what was measured: the healthcheck says nothing during the conversion, its length depends on the machine and the number of files, and a fixed bound printed a wrong hint on a Hub that went on to finish by itself.

**Rejected alternatives.**
- *Moving every Hub at its next setup run:* decision 068, unchanged by anything measured.
- *No copy, relying on what 2.x leaves:* gone after 14 and 30 days, and putting it back by hand is three moves, the third easy to miss.
- *Copying while the stack runs:* the 1.x database may be copied mid-write.
- *Copying only the 1.x database:* the way back also needs the old `config.xml` and must take `index-v2` away; the whole directory is one copy and one rename.
- *A longer `start_period` in the healthcheck:* the healthcheck passes during the conversion anyway, so it would change nothing.
- *Setup restoring the copy after a failed conversion by itself:* the doctrine is pause, explain, let the owner act (decision 002); a failed conversion leaves the 1.x database untouched, and a new run converts again.
- *A `[y/N]` like the image offer:* it follows a `[y/N]` one line above, and this answer cannot be undone by running setup again.
