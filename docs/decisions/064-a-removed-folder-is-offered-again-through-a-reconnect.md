# 064 — A removed folder is offered again through a reconnect, and a dial whose handshake failed is tried once more from another port

**Context.** A vault removed on the iPhone while its peer stayed connected was not offered again until the app restarted (#316, seen on a device, the same on 2.1.0). The notices of #270 and #315, the merge question and the troubleshooting page all send people that way: remove the vault, accept its share again. The engine notes an offer only when a peer's cluster configuration arrives (`ccHandleFolders`), and a peer sends one when a connection is set up or when its own configuration changes. A removal here sends *our* configuration to the peer and changes nothing on it, so nothing came back. `TestIssue316_ARemovedVaultsShareIsOfferedAgain` shows it against a real peer.

**Decision.** (1) Fork patch `006-reannounce-after-folder-removal`: when a folder is removed, the engine closes its connections to the devices the folder was shared with. Both sides dial again at once and announce their folders on the new connection, and the removed one becomes pending. The close comes after the commit has discarded what was pending for the removed folder, and it reads the connections at that moment, so no announcement can arrive early and be discarded with the rest. Those devices get no cluster configuration from that commit: it would go out just ahead of the close and can hold the close message back. Nothing is written for it. (2) Fork patch `007-retry-without-port-reuse-after-failed-handshake`: a TCP dial whose TLS handshake fails is tried once more without reusing the listening port. (3) The app is unchanged: a removed folder's ID stays in `userRemovedFolderIDs` until an explicit accept, so the offer that now returns while the app is open is listed and waits for a tap (002, #52; `Issue316RemovedShareTests`).

**Why 007 belongs to it.** A close seen by both ends makes both dial in the same instant, each from its listening port to the other's. TCP can join two such attempts into one connection ("simultaneous open"); both ends then speak TLS as the client and both handshakes fail. Each side tries again after the same interval and meets the other the same way: against the real peer the trace showed `tls: received unexpected handshake message of type *tls.clientHelloMsg` at three attempts in a row, 8 and 16 seconds apart, and no connection for the rest of the test. Upstream has the same exposure wherever it closes a connection on purpose; 006 would have made it part of an everyday action. A dial from a port of its own cannot be joined with one coming in. A handshake that failed for another reason fails again and the first error is returned; the dial's deadline bounds both tries.

**What it costs, measured.** The peer's other folders stop for the reconnect and go on: 1.0 to 1.3 seconds on loopback (40 runs of the three tests with both patches on 2026-11-14, none failed; before 007 and the change to the rig named below, about one run of the real-peer tests in seven did). The engine dials a device at once after a close only a few times in a row — three forced dials inside two minutes, then none for five — and a double dial uses two of them. From the second or third removal in a row the reconnect therefore waits for the next regular dial, `reconnectIntervalS` after the last one. With the interval an install has, five vaults removed one after another, each once the one before was offered again, came back after 1.3 s, 1.3 s, 60 s, 60 s and 60 s (`VAULTSYNC_BRIDGE_ISSUE316_RECONNECT_S=60`); the test in the suite runs with five seconds. Vaults removed while disconnected are all announced on that one reconnect. The app shows a device as "Connecting…" for 30 seconds before it reports it disconnected, so in that case the device of a vault that stays can show as disconnected for up to half a minute before it is back.

**Rejected alternatives.** *Noting the offer from what the engine remembers of the peer's last announcement, without a reconnect:* it would be an entry the app made up — the engine keeps a folder's state per device, not the label or the flags of the announcement — and it would stand even if the peer had stopped sharing in the meantime. *Pausing and resuming the device:* two writes to the configuration, and an app killed between them leaves the peer paused. *Stopping and starting the engine:* #302, and it takes every peer down. *Changing the texts to "reopen VaultSync":* the fallback if the behaviour could not be fixed. *Delaying our own redial so the peer's comes first:* changes the timing of every reconnect and helps only while the other side has no such delay. *Closing the extra connection of a double dial without counting it against the redial limit:* that limit is the engine's guard against a connection that keeps dropping, and a loop of removals should meet it too.

**Not covered.** A peer that is not connected when the vault is removed announces the folder when it next connects, as before. The engine closes a connection by aborting it (`SO_LINGER 0`, upstream), so a peer learns of a close from the close message or from the reset, and when it gets neither it holds its end until its own ping or timeout notices, and refuses every new connection as a second one until then. The tests met that on a Mac whose firewall runs in stealth mode, for a connection dialed from a listener that was closed a moment later: nothing was left on the port to answer what arrived for it. The rig moves the engine to a loopback port right after start and now waits for the first listener to close before it pairs; an install keeps the listener it starts with. That exposure is upstream's for every close, a removal now among them, and it ends by itself.
