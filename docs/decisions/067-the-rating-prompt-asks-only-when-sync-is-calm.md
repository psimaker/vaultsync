# 067 — The rating prompt is the system's, and asks only when sync is calm

**Context.** The app never asked for an App Store rating (#327). An ask at the wrong moment — over a consent question, while a share waits, before the first vault arrived — interrupts exactly the decisions this app exists to slow down, and an app that asks while something is broken asks the wrong people at the wrong time.

**Decision.** The app calls the system's `requestReview` and nothing else: no dialog of its own before it, no second ask, no link that leaves the app. `ReviewPromptGate` (pure, `ViewModels/ReviewPromptGate.swift`) decides; the view only feeds it and calls. It asks once sync has completed on three different calendar days (`requiredSyncDays`), at most once per app version, and only when all of these hold at the moment of the call: the engine runs without an error, the Obsidian folder is connected, at least one vault syncs, `unresolvedIssues` is empty, no folder is unreachable or found to be a copy, no pending share waits — one the user set aside included —, nothing is syncing, every vault row reads "Up to date" (none paused, in error, waiting for its first sync, holding conflicts), no sheet, alert, pushed screen, other tab or pairing link about to open is in front of the Sync tab — the shell's own presentations and, through `HubLinkGate.somethingIsPresented`, a child view's —, neither the Relay offer nor the notification primer is on the dashboard, and the app has been active for 30 seconds (`minimumActiveSeconds`). The check runs after a sync completed while the app runs: three seconds later, and not before those 30 seconds — the engine rescans on every start and the history backfills that completion, so a check at launch would ask at launch. Days are keyed in the Gregorian calendar and the device's time zone. The record (`reviewPrompt.v1` in UserDefaults: the last three sync days, the version asked for and the day of the ask) is written before the call, because iOS reports nothing back; an ask clears the days and no day up to the day of the ask counts again, so the next version needs three new ones. The unit-test host, the UI fixtures and Debug builds never ask (a Debug build does with `-review-prompt-in-debug YES`): a development build shows the prompt on every call, over a UI-test driver's taps too.

**Why.** The system prompt is the only one iOS limits for the app (three showings a year) and the only one that cannot be made to nag. A day counts by the date of the completion itself (the history's date loaded at launch too), in the device's time zone. "Sync completed" is the #94 rule: a folder reaching idle with nothing to fetch while a peer is connected — so in practice three days on which VaultSync was opened with a working peer, not three days on which files moved. That is the intended meaning of "sync has worked for a while"; counting transferred files would keep the ask from people whose notes are already in step.

**Rejected alternatives.** A dialog of our own first ("Do you like VaultSync?") that routes unhappy answers elsewhere: the issue rules it out, and it is an ask of its own. Asking at launch or on becoming active: interrupts the first thing people came to do. Keeping a second list of the shell's sheets for the prompt: it drifts from the Hub link's list, which is reused. Counting syncs that complete in the background: the app is not on screen then, and the foreground count is enough. A higher day count or a minimum age of the install: three days already leave out the first-setup week's troubles, and the attention rule carries the rest.
