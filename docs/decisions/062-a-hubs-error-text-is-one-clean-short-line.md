# 062 — A Hub's error text is one clean, short line, made in the pairing client

**Context.** When a pairing request fails, the pairing client hands out the text of the answer: `APIError.Body` for an answer with an error status, and `HubPayload.Error` for what a Hub reports inside its encrypted answer. `vaultsync-hub pair` and `vaultsync pair` print both, the menu shows the second program's words in a dialog, and the iPhone's bridge passes them to the app's log. The first text comes from whatever answered at the address that was dialled, before the key exchange; the second from a Hub that knows the code, and it passes on what that Hub's Syncthing said. Neither was cleaned or bounded (#319): a terminal sequence, U+202E, line breaks, up to the 64 KiB an answer may be.

**Decision.** (1) `errorText` in `hub/pairing` makes such a text one line. A run of white space that holds a tab, a line break or a line or paragraph separator becomes one space; other white space stays as it was sent, except at either end. What `SanitizeName` drops from a name is dropped (061), and the rest is cut to 300 bytes between two characters, with "…" at the end. (2) The client applies it to both texts before it hands them out, and reads the kind of a refusal from the cleaned and cut text: what is judged is what is shown. A refusal that a device tells apart by its words therefore carries them within its first 300 bytes. (3) An error stays an error. An answer whose text has nothing to show reads as its HTTP status does, like an answer without a text; an encrypted answer's error becomes a fixed phrase, because callers tell a refusal from a share by whether `Error` is empty.

**Why.** In the client, so that no program that prints one of these two texts has to remember — the reason the Hub's name is cleaned there. A space for a line break, because two lines must stay two words: for the reader, and for the phrases `FailureKind` and `Handshake` look for ("rejected", "code has expired", "locked"). Other white space stays, because a Hub quotes names in its texts — the vault it was asked for, a vault's label — and a name may be written with two spaces or a wide one. 300 bytes, because in the setup a Hub ships with a refusal is at most 100 bytes, and the Hub's own words in front of an answer of its Syncthing, which it cuts at 200 bytes, are about 260. `TestIssue319_TheHubsOwnRefusalsArriveWhole` provokes every refusal that is told apart by its words on the real server and holds it against what the client hands out, `TestIssue319_ASyncthingAnswerBehindTheHubsWordsArrivesWhole` does the same for the longest of the Hub's texts, and `FuzzIssue319_ErrorText` holds the rule against any bytes: a refusal reworded past the bound fails there. Nothing on the wire changes: Hubs and devices with and without this change pair with each other as before.

**Rejected alternatives.** Cleaning in each program that prints: three places today, and the next one forgets. Reading the kind from the text as it came and cleaning it only for display: two texts for one answer, and the kind would hang on words nobody sees. Dropping a line break as a name does: "wrong" and "attempts" on two lines would read as one word. Making every run of white space one space: asked for "My  Notes" on a Hub that has "My Notes", a device read that no vault is named "My Notes", and it stops no padding (below). Leaving the encrypted answer's error as it came because that Hub knows the code: the same programs print it, and the Hub passes on text it did not write. Cleaning on the Hub before it sends: the sender of an answer is the one party a device cannot rely on. Keeping the end of a cut text as well, or a second, shorter bound for an answer before the key exchange: more edges for a text no default setup produces, and a constant for display that would be a contract twice.

**Not covered.** Inside the bound a text can still be spread over rows by padding — spaces, or characters that draw a blank — behind the command's own first row; no list of such characters is complete (061). What the HTTP client itself says about an answer that is no proper HTTP, and what it logs when bytes follow an answer, is quoted, so no control character passes, but it is not cut (#323). A text of more than 300 bytes loses its end, and the tags of an emoji flag stay whatever their number (#324). A vault's label from a Hub (#320).
