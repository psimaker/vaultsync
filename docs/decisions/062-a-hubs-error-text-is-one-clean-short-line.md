# 062 — A Hub's error text is one clean, short line, made in the pairing client

**Context.** When a pairing request fails, the pairing client hands out the text of the answer: `APIError.Body` for an answer with an error status, and `HubPayload.Error` for what a Hub reports inside its encrypted answer. `vaultsync-hub pair` and `vaultsync pair` print both, the menu shows the second program's words in a dialog, and the iPhone's bridge passes them to the app's log. The first text comes from whatever answered at the address that was dialled, before the key exchange; the second from a Hub that knows the code, and it passes on what that Hub's Syncthing said. Neither was cleaned or bounded (#319): a terminal sequence, U+202E, line breaks, up to the 64 KiB an answer may be.

**Decision.** (1) `errorText` in `hub/pairing` makes such a text one line. A run of white space that holds a tab, a line break or a line or paragraph separator becomes one space; other white space stays as it was sent, except at either end. What `SanitizeName` drops from a name is dropped (061), and the rest is cut to 300 bytes between two characters, with "…" at the end. (2) The client applies it to both texts before it hands them out, and reads the kind of a refusal from the cleaned and cut text: what is judged is what is shown. A refusal that a device tells apart by its words therefore carries them within its first 300 bytes. (3) An error stays an error. An answer whose text has nothing to show reads as its HTTP status does, like an answer without a text; an encrypted answer's error becomes a fixed phrase, because callers tell a refusal from a share by whether `Error` is empty. (4) The client's other errors about an answer are cut by the same rule, as a whole: what Go's HTTP client says of an answer that is no proper HTTP — it quotes the line it could not read, as long as it was —, of an answer that broke off, and a body that is no JSON. The text is one line of at most 300 bytes with the library's own words in front; the error itself stays under it (`Unwrap`), and a failure is sorted as before (#323). (5) The client reads at most 64 KiB of an answer's head, opens a connection of its own for each request, and reads a connection only once its request is written on it. Go's transport writes the bytes that reach it while it waits for no answer to the program's log: behind an answer on a connection it keeps for the next request, and before it has counted the request it is about to send. With these, nothing that answers at the address reaches that log. A read that waits for the request ends when its read deadline passes, or when the connection is closed. The connection is closed before that read is let go, so it takes nothing that was sent before the request, whatever order the transport closes in.

**Why.** In the client, so that no program that prints one of these two texts has to remember — the reason the Hub's name is cleaned there. A space for a line break, because two lines must stay two words: for the reader, and for the phrases `FailureKind` and `Handshake` look for ("rejected", "code has expired", "locked"). Other white space stays, because a Hub quotes names in its texts — the vault it was asked for, a vault's label — and a name may be written with two spaces or a wide one. 300 bytes, because in the setup a Hub ships with a refusal is at most 100 bytes, and the Hub's own words in front of an answer of its Syncthing, which it cuts at 200 bytes, are about 260. `TestIssue319_TheHubsOwnRefusalsArriveWhole` provokes every refusal that is told apart by its words on the real server and holds it against what the client hands out, `TestIssue319_ASyncthingAnswerBehindTheHubsWordsArrivesWhole` does the same for the longest of the Hub's texts, and `FuzzIssue319_ErrorText` holds the rule against any bytes: a refusal reworded past the bound fails there. Nothing on the wire changes: Hubs and devices with and without this change pair with each other as before. The library's error keeps its chain under the cut text because every caller sorts a failure by it: `FailureKind`, the desktop agent's and the bridge's sorting of a provision whose outcome may be unknown (a refused dial, an address outside the local network), the Mac's local-network hint (no route to host). Its words stay because they say what went wrong: nothing listened, the connection broke, something that is no Hub answered. 64 KiB, because a Hub's head is under 200 bytes and a reverse proxy in front of one adds a few KiB, against the library's default of 10 MiB, which it reads and holds before it gives up; the bound is on what is read, the text is cut anyway. A connection per request, because a pairing is a handful of requests on the local network, and the check at the socket for an address from outside now runs for each of them. Reading only after writing, because the transport counts a request before it writes it: a service that greets first, as an SSH server does, reached the log 5 times in 1000 on loopback, and every time once that moment was held open — keeping no connection between requests does not close it. The client speaks plain HTTP only; a TLS handshake writes first, so the rule would change nothing there. `TestIssue323_…` in `hub/pairing`, `hub`, `desktop` and `go/bridge` hold each path against the real client, and every way a request fails is sorted there as it was.

**Rejected alternatives.** Cleaning in each program that prints: three places today, and the next one forgets. Reading the kind from the text as it came and cleaning it only for display: two texts for one answer, and the kind would hang on words nobody sees. Dropping a line break as a name does: "wrong" and "attempts" on two lines would read as one word. Making every run of white space one space: asked for "My  Notes" on a Hub that has "My Notes", a device read that no vault is named "My Notes", and it stops no padding (below). Leaving the encrypted answer's error as it came because that Hub knows the code: the same programs print it, and the Hub passes on text it did not write. Cleaning on the Hub before it sends: the sender of an answer is the one party a device cannot rely on. Keeping the end of a cut text as well, or a second, shorter bound for an answer before the key exchange: more edges for a text no default setup produces, and a constant for display that would be a contract twice. Discarding the standard logger in the programs, as the iPhone's bridge does: it is the whole program's, and a library that changed it for its callers would change it for everything they link. A fixed phrase in place of the library's words: it hides whether nothing listened, the connection broke or something else answered, the first question when pairing fails. A head bound small enough to keep the library's text short: the library quotes a head of 4 KiB in up to 16 KiB, and a smaller bound refuses a Hub behind a proxy that paired before.

**Not covered.** Inside the bound a text can still be spread over rows by padding — spaces, or characters that draw a blank — behind the command's own first row; no list of such characters is complete (061). An answer whose head is longer than 64 KiB is refused like one that is no proper HTTP. The other HTTP clients of the same programs — the ones that talk to the local Syncthing or download it — keep the library's defaults (#323). A text of more than 300 bytes loses its end, and the tags of an emoji flag stay whatever their number (#324). A vault's label from a Hub (#320).
